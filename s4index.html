Porter's Five Forces Strategic Industry Summary

Michael Porter's framework assesses structural profit potential by analyzing external commercial pressures.

1. Threat of New Entrants

Determined by production economies of scale, strict capital barriers, and intellectual protections keeping rivals out.

2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers

Spikes high when supply pipelines are consolidated, switching vendors is costly, and alternate inputs are unavailable.

3. Bargaining Power of Buyers

Driven by customer purchasing volume concentration and the absolute availability of identical alternative products.

4. Threat of Substitutes

Evaluates competitive solutions outside direct categories that carry better price-to-performance trade-offs.

5. Intensity of Competitive Rivalry (Central Hub)

Fueled by industry growth rates, number of equal-sized competitors, and exit barriers that trigger intense margin-slashing pricing wars.
